Lesson 4 · About 10 minutes

Tips: debug and think bigger

Things will break. That is normal, and the AI can fix most of it if you show it what you see. Then raise your sights.

  • Goal: fix problems faster, then aim higher

Part 1: Debug by showing, not guessing

Debugging means finding and fixing what went wrong. You do not need to find the cause yourself. Your job is to give the AI good evidence.

1. Take a screenshot

Paste it straight into the chat. Windows: Win + Shift + S. Mac: Cmd + Shift + 4. A picture often says what words miss.

2. Copy the exact error

If you see an error message, paste all of it. Do not retype or summarize it.

3. Say what you expected

“I clicked Submit and expected a thank-you message. Nothing happened.” Expected versus actual is the fastest clue.

4. Ask the AI to explain

“Explain what went wrong in plain words.” You learn something each time, and you can tell whether the fix makes sense.

5. Ask the AI to do it

Let it find the cause, fix it, and return the complete file. Ask it to list what it changed.

6. Go back if you are stuck

After three failed fixes, return to the last version that worked, or start a fresh chat and paste your current code.

See the difference

The same problem, reported two ways.

✗ Weak

“It's broken. Fix it.”

The AI has to guess what “it” is, what broke, and what working looks like.

✓ Strong

“On my phone, the Submit button does nothing. I expected a thank-you message. Screenshot attached. Here is my full index.html.”

Where, what happened, what you expected, and the evidence. The AI can go straight to the cause.

Use this template whenever something breaks:

Debug prompt

Something is not working. What I did: [clicked the Submit button on my phone]. What I expected: [a thank-you message]. What happened instead: [nothing]. I attached a screenshot and pasted my full code below. Find the cause, explain it in plain words, then give me the complete corrected index.html and list what you changed.

Part 2: Be more ambitious

Most people ask AI for too little. Asking costs you nothing, and the worst outcome is that it does not work and you learn where the limit is.

  1. Level 1

    A page

    What you built in the DIY lesson: information on a screen.

  2. Level 2

    A tool

    Something that does a job: a calculator, a quiz, a tracker, a booking form.

  3. Level 3

    Connected to what you already use

    A dashboard that reads your spreadsheet. A page that fills in your existing template. Ask the AI how to connect it.

  4. Level 4

    A workflow that runs for you

    Several steps joined together, replacing a task you repeat every week.

Ways to push further

Think creatively

Ask “What else could this do?” and “What would a professional add?” Let the AI suggest ideas you would not have thought of.

Connect your spreadsheet

Paste a few sample rows and ask for a page that charts or searches them. Then ask how to link it to the live file.

Start from software you already use

Name the tools in your day, such as email, calendar or a learning platform, and ask what could be built around them.

Try it and test its limits

Ask for the ambitious version first. If it fails, scale back one step. You will be surprised how often it works.

Idea prompt

Every day I use [Excel, Outlook, and Google Calendar] for [my job as a training coordinator]. Suggest five small tools you could build for me that would save time. For each one, tell me what it does, how it would connect to the software I already use, and how hard it is to build. Then ask me which one to start with.

One caution. Do not paste passwords, private data about other people, or confidential work files into a chatbot. Use made-up sample data while you build.

Quick check

Question 1 of 2A button on your page does nothing. Which message will get the fastest fix?

Question 2 of 2You have a working page and want it to be more useful. What is the most productive next move?

Key takeaways

  • Vibecoding is directing: you describe, the AI builds, you steer.
  • Start with a beginner-friendly tool or the chatbot you already use.
  • One index.html and a free GitHub repo are enough to publish.
  • When it breaks, show evidence: screenshot, exact error, expected versus actual.
  • Ask for more than you think is possible, then test it.
